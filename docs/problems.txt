Mutual Fund FAQs (Facts-Only Q&A)
Milestone brief
Build a small FAQ assistant that answers facts about mutual fund schemes—e.g., expense ratio, exit load, minimum SIP, lock-in (ELSS), riskometer, benchmark, and how to download statements—using only official public pages. Every answer must include one source link. No advice.

Who this helps
Retail users comparing schemes; support/content teams answering repetitive MF questions.

What you must build
Scope your corpus: Pick one AMC and 3–5 schemes under it (e.g., one large-cap, one flexi-cap, one ELSS).

Collect 15–25 public pages from AMC/SEBI/AMFI (factsheets, KIM/SID, scheme FAQs, fee/charges pages, riskometer/benchmark notes, statement/tax-doc guides).

FAQ assistant (working prototype):

Answers factual queries only (e.g., “Expense ratio of ?”, “ELSS lock-in?”, “Minimum SIP?”, “Exit load?”, “Riskometer/benchmark?”, “How to download capital-gains statement?”).

Shows one clear citation link in every answer.

Refuses opinionated/portfolio questions (e.g., “Should I buy/sell?”) with a polite, facts-only message and a relevant educational link.

Tiny UI: welcome line + 3 example questions and a note: “Facts-only. No investment advice.”

Key constraints
Public sources only. No screenshots of the app back-end; no third-party blogs as sources.

No PII. Do not accept/store PAN, Aadhaar, account numbers, OTPs, emails, or phone numbers.

No performance claims. Don’t compute/compare returns; link to the official factsheet if asked.

Clarity & transparency. Keep answers ≤3 sentences; add “Last updated from sources: ”.

Multi-Agent System: Support Response Desk
Pick the ame product you picked in Milestone 1.

Brief
Build a multi-agent system that takes a real user question or complaint and drafts a support reply. Three specialist agents run in parallel: one finds the facts, one checks whether this is a known pattern, one checks the draft won't get you in trouble. An orchestrator merges them into a reply and an internal routing note. A reviewer blocks anything that breaks the facts-only rule before a human ever sees it.

Where this goes
Milestone 1 gave you a cited FAQ corpus. Milestone 2 gave you clustered review themes and an approval-gated MCP workflow. This wires both into one system that answers a specific user instead of writing a weekly summary.

The problem
A support agent answering "why was I charged an exit load" needs three things at once, and they don't come from the same place.

The fact lives in a scheme document. Whether forty other users asked the same thing this month decides if this is a reply or an escalation. And the draft has to not give investment advice, not quote a return figure, and not repeat back a PAN the user pasted into their complaint.

One model asked to do all three does the first and forgets the rest. It writes a fluent, uncited paragraph ending in "you may want to consider staying invested longer", which is advice, and now you have a compliance problem instead of a support ticket.

The architecture
architechture


What you build
Input - A user question or complaint, typed in or pulled from your Milestone 2 reviews CSV.
Three agents, in parallel - None of them waits on or reads another's output.
Facts Agent : Retrieves the answer from your Milestone 1 corpus and returns it with one citation link. When the corpus doesn't cover it, it returns "not covered" and names what it searched. It doesn't fall back on general knowledge.
Pattern Agent : Checks the complaint against your Milestone 2 themes. Returns whether this is recurring, rising or a one-off, with the count of reviews behind it and the window you used.
Compliance Agent : Scans the incoming message and the outgoing draft for advice language, performance or return claims, and PII. PAN, Aadhaar, account numbers, phone numbers, emails and OTPs all get flagged and redacted before anything is stored.
Orchestrator - Two outputs. A reply draft of three sentences or fewer, facts-only, with its citation link and a "last checked" date. And an internal routing note saying which team it goes to and why, using the Pattern Agent's signal to set priority.
When the agents disagree, say Facts has a clean answer but Pattern shows forty people asking the same thing this week, the orchestrator says so rather than sending the reply and closing the ticket.

Reviewer - Checks the draft before a human sees it. An uncited factual claim, anything that reads as advice, and any surviving PII are hard fails, sent back to the orchestrator once with the specific failure named. If the second attempt still fails, show the draft with the flag on it and don't offer the send.
Refinement - The human pushes back and only the affected agents re-run. "Shorter" touches the orchestrator. "They're asking about a different scheme" touches Facts. "Check if this is the fee issue from last week" touches Pattern. Log which agents fired.
Approval-gated MCP - Same gate as Milestone 2. Nothing moves without a human clicking approve. After approval, append the record to your doc and create the email draft. No auto-send.
Rules
Facts-only. No recommendations, no buy or sell language, no return or performance figures
Every factual claim carries one citation link. No link means it doesn't ship
No PII stored anywhere, including in logs and agent state
Public sources only, same corpus as Milestone 1
Three agents run in parallel, shown in your diagram and demo
Reviewer rework capped at one round, refinement at five
Reply drafts are three sentences or fewer with a "last checked" date
Before you submit
- Run 20 real complaints from your reviews CSV. Confirm all three agents returned structured output on every one, and that every reply either carries a citation or says the corpus doesn't cover it.

- Check the branches Feed in one complaint the corpus answers cleanly, one it can't, one matching a recurring theme, and one containing a PAN or phone number. Log which agents fired on each and confirm the reviewer caught the PII.

- Try to make it give advice Ask whether you should redeem, whether a scheme is good, what returns to expect. Rephrase, then ask again later in the same session. It should refuse every time.


Graduation project: The Support Desk
Pick the same product you picked in Milestone 1.

Objective
Build a deployed system that runs the support and product loop for one Indian investment platform. Reviews come in and become themes. User questions come in and become cited, compliance-checked reply drafts. A weekly pulse goes out to the product team. Nothing leaves without a human approving it.

The problem
Two teams are solving halves of the same problem and neither can see the other half.

Support answers the same fee question forty times a month, writing it fresh each time, sometimes getting it wrong. Product reads a review dashboard that tells them "pricing confusion is up" without telling them which fee, or what support has been telling people about it.

The connective tissue is data neither team has in one place: what the official documents actually say, what users are actually confused about, and how often. That's what you're building.

What you bring forward
Milestone 1 gave you a cited FAQ corpus over official pages, with a facts-only refusal. Milestone 2 gave you review clustering, the weekly pulse, the fee explainer and an approval-gated MCP workflow. Milestone 3 gave you the three-agent response desk with its reviewer.

What's left is running them as one system on a schedule, the interface, evaluations over the assembled whole, and deployment.

What you build
A deployed web application with two loops.

1. The weekly loop. Ingest your reviews CSV, cluster into a maximum of five themes, identify the top three, and pull the recurring fee or charge confusion. Generate the weekly pulse, 250 words or fewer, with supporting quotes and three action ideas. Generate the fee explainer, six bullets or fewer, facts-only, with two official source links and a "last checked" date. Both go through the approval gate before the doc append and the email draft.

2. The response loop. A queue of incoming user questions. Each one runs through the three agents in parallel, the orchestrator, and the reviewer, producing a reply draft with its citation and an internal routing note. A human approves, edits or rejects each draft.

3. The connection between them. This is what neither milestone did on its own. When the response loop sees the same question five times in a week, it shows up in the weekly pulse. When the weekly loop identifies a fee confusion, the explainer becomes the canonical answer the response loop reuses instead of drafting fresh. Build this link and show it working.

4. Scope enforcement. Facts-only across both loops. No recommendations, no buy or sell language, no return or performance figures, no PII stored anywhere including logs and agent state. Enforced in code, not only in prompts.

The interface
A queue view of incoming questions with their draft replies, citations and any reviewer flags. A weekly pulse view with the themes, quotes and counts behind them. A sources panel showing where every factual claim came from, with its last-checked date. An approval screen listing exactly what will be written and where, before anything is written. And a way to see the canonical fee explainers the response loop is reusing.

Rules
Every factual claim carries one source link from your Milestone 1 corpus
Public sources only. No third-party blogs, no app back-end screenshots
When the corpus doesn't cover a question, the system says so and names what it searched
Every theme traces to review ids, and every count states the window it covers
No PII anywhere in the dataset, the interface, the logs or agent state
Nothing is written or sent without a human approving that specific action
Reply drafts stay at three sentences or fewer, the pulse at 250 words, the explainer at six bullets
Evaluations
Three checks minimum, running over the assembled system.

Feasibility. Every question in the queue produces a draft or an explicit "not covered". Every theme carries review ids and a stated window. The pulse stays inside its word cap and the explainer inside its bullet cap. The weekly loop completes without manual intervention once triggered.

Correctness of edits. A human edit to a draft changes only that draft. Approving one action doesn't trigger another. Rejecting a draft leaves the queue otherwise untouched. Test each and diff the state before and after.

Grounding and compliance. Sample fifteen claims across both loops and confirm each traces to a source link or a review id. Feed in questions the corpus can't answer and confirm it says so. Then run advice requests, return-figure requests, and messages containing a PAN, an account number and a phone number, in both loops, rephrased and repeated. Confirm every one is refused or redacted, and that nothing sensitive reached the logs.
